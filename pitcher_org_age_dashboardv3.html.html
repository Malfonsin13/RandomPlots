Conclusions

Same macro pattern as before: the biggest buildup jump is 19→20 at 19.9 IP on average for same-org repeaters. Then 20→21 is still positive at 11.9. By 21→22 it flattens to -1.5.

Most aggressive same-org buildup among orgs with 5+ repeat pairs: TB at +18.5 IP. Most conservative: NYM at -3.9 IP.

With your exact strict-return rule — baseline 60+ IP, next year only counts if IP is higher — the best qualified org on pure return rate is MIA at 58.3%.

If you only give the org credit when the player returns and increases workload in the same org, MIA leads at 58.3%.

Biggest same-org retained workload gain belongs to LAA: 189.3 extra IP from players who actually returned and moved forward.

Overview is still the same: COL is the total-volume king at 3931.3 IP, while KC is the best large-sample efficiency org at 15.86 pitches/IP.

Young-for-level and still good at the org level: DET had the best weighted xPitchRV/P among orgs younger than their level context (0.0126).

The youngest meaningful org that still held up competitively was MIA: -0.83 years younger than level context and still positive by xPitchRV/P.

Best young-results team signal: Lakeland Flying Tigers (DET, AFX). Biggest young-workload team signal: Wilson Warbirds (MIL, AFX).

Strict-return cohorts use 2023→2024 and 2024→2025 only. I exclude age-22 baseline rows from that specific return math because a 2024 age-22 pitcher is structurally unfair in an 18–22 export when you try to find him again in 2025/2026.
